Haircuts mix many hard things at once: noise, buzzing, itchy hair, a stranger close to the face, and sitting still. A little planning can make the day shorter and calmer for everyone.

## 1. Before

- Show photo steps the night before and in the morning: chair, cape, water spray, scissors, clippers, mirror, done.
- Choose a quiet time. Call ahead and ask for the first slot of the day or a slot when the shop is empty.
- Ask the barber to read a short note about your child (the brief from Dayline works for this).
- Try the sounds at home: play a clipper sound on the phone, let your child hold an electric toothbrush to feel the buzz.
- Wash hair at home first if water on the head is hard at the shop.

## 2. During

- Ear defenders on before the clippers start.
- Let your child sit on your lap if that helps; a cape can go over both of you.
- Count down out loud ("10 snips, then a break") and keep to it.
- A favourite video or toy in their hands.
- Short is fine. Agree with the barber what "good enough" looks like before you start.

## 3. After

- Brush off loose hair straight away, change the T-shirt, or have a quick bath; itchy hair is often the worst part.
- A reward your child chose before you went.
- Note what worked in Dayline so next time starts from there.

## If it goes badly

- Stop. Finishing another day is fine.
- Move somewhere quiet and use what calms your child (Help now in Dayline shows your own steps).
- Next time, try a home haircut or a barber who visits homes, and shorter sessions.
