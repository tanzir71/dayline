Ramadan changes the whole day at home: meal times move, nights get later, and the house is busy at iftar and quiet in the afternoon. Your child's own meals and sleep can stay steady even when the house changes around them.

## 1. Before Ramadan

- Tell your child what will change, with a picture schedule: sehri, the day, iftar, prayers, bedtime.
- Decide which parts of your child's day stay the same: their meal times, school or therapy, bedtime steps.
- If your child takes medicines, read the guide on medicine times in Ramadan and talk to the prescriber before Ramadan starts.
- Agree with family who helps when: who stays with your child at iftar, who keeps the house calm at sehri.

## 2. During Ramadan

- Keep your child's meals regular, even if the family eats at different times. Offer water often through the day.
- Plan for iftar: it is often loud and busy, with strong smells and many people. Offer a quiet corner, headphones, or your child's own plate at a calm table.
- Keep bedtime steps the same, even if bedtime is later on some nights.
- If the house wakes for sehri, keep your child's room dark and quiet so they can sleep through.
- Give your child a role if they enjoy it: setting the table, handing out dates, counting down to iftar.

## 3. Towards Eid

- Start moving back to the usual routine a few days before Ramadan ends.
- Get ready for Eid too: new clothes, visits, travel and crowds each have a guide in Dayline.

## If it goes badly

- Go back to fewer changes. A simpler routine for your child is fine.
- Take breaks from busy gatherings, and use what calms your child (Help now in Dayline has the steps).
- If your child wants to try fasting, talk to their doctor first, especially if they take medicines or have a health condition.
