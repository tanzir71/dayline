A meltdown and a shutdown both come from overload: too much noise, change, demand, pain or tiredness. They look different, but neither is bad behaviour, and in both your child needs less input and more safety.

## 1. What they can look like

- A meltdown goes outward: crying, shouting, hitting, throwing, running, or hurting themselves. Your child cannot simply stop it.
- A shutdown goes inward: going quiet or still, not answering, staring, lying down, or seeming far away. It is easy to miss, or to mistake for ignoring you.
- Some children move between the two. The early signs are often similar: covering ears, pacing, rocking more, going quiet, or repeating words.

## 2. In a meltdown

- Keep everyone safe. Move hard or sharp things away and give space.
- Stop talking, or keep it to a few soft words. This is not the time to reason, warn or ask questions.
- Lower the input: lights down, noise down, fewer people watching.
- Offer, without forcing, what usually calms your child: deep pressure, a blanket, a favourite object, water. Help now in Dayline has these steps.

## 3. In a shutdown

- Stay nearby and calm. Do not push your child to talk, look at you or move.
- Make the space quiet and dim, and drop all demands.
- Give time. Check gently that your child is breathing comfortably and is safe.
- As your child starts to come back, quietly offer water or a snack.

## 4. Afterwards

- Rest comes first. Your child may be very tired or remember little.
- Later, think about what came just before: noise, hunger, pain, tiredness, a change of plan. Note it in Dayline so patterns can show over time.
- Look after yourself too. These moments are hard for parents.

## When to get help

- If you cannot keep your child or others safe, or your child is hurt or has trouble breathing, call your emergency number (999 in Bangladesh).
- If hard moments are happening more often or getting worse, talk to your child's doctor. A sudden change can be a sign of pain or illness.
