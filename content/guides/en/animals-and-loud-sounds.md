Eid ul-Adha brings animals to the street or home, busy cattle markets, loud sounds, strong smells and a very different day. Some autistic children love the animals and others find it frightening, so plan ahead and let your child take part in the way that suits them.

## 1. Before

- Show photos and short videos of cows and goats, the market and the street on Eid, and talk about what will happen.
- Decide whether your child needs to go to the cattle market. If they go, choose a quieter time and a short visit, with ear defenders.
- Explain where the animal will stay, and what sounds and smells to expect.
- Plan where your child will be during the sacrifice. Many families choose a quiet room, a relative's home or a calm activity indoors.

## 2. With the animals

- If your child likes animals, let them watch, feed or touch from a safe distance, with an adult close by.
- Teach a few simple safety rules: stay next to the adult, never walk behind an animal, wash hands after touching.
- If your child is scared, keep them at a distance or indoors. Watching from a window counts.

## 3. On Eid day

- Keep windows closed and a fan or music on in your child's room if the sounds or smells are hard.
- Give your child a familiar activity and familiar food while the house is busy.
- Think ahead about blood and meat. You can keep your child away, or explain simply and calmly if they ask.
- Plan visits and new clothes with the matching Dayline guides, and keep breaks in the day.

## If it goes badly

- Move your child to the quietest room, use fewer words, and offer what calms them (Help now in Dayline has the steps).
- It is fine for your child to skip parts of the day.
- If your child sleeps badly or seems upset for days afterwards, keep routines steady, and talk to their doctor if it does not settle.
