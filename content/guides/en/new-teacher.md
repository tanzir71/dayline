A new teacher means a new voice, new rules and new ways of doing things. For many autistic children that is a big change, even when the school stays the same, and preparing both your child and the teacher makes the first weeks easier.

## 1. Before the first day

- Ask the school for the teacher's name and a photo. Show it often and say, "This is your new teacher."
- If you can, visit the classroom when it is empty. Find your child's seat, the toilet and the place for bags.
- Make a short picture story: new class, new teacher, and what stays the same (friends, uniform, lunch box, pickup).
- Meet the teacher before term or in the first week, and share a one-page brief about your child. The guide on briefing a new teacher explains what to include.

## 2. The first days

- Keep the morning routine the same as before, so only one thing is new.
- Ask if your child can arrive a few minutes early, before the room fills up, or have shorter days at first.
- Give your child something familiar to hold or to keep in their bag.
- Agree with the teacher on a sign your child can use to ask for a break, and a quiet place to go.

## 3. Building the relationship

- Ask the teacher to share one thing that went well each day, by note or message.
- Tell the teacher what works at home: a word, a song, a reward, a way of giving instructions.
- Share your child's interests. A teacher who knows about trains, cricket or drawing has an easy way to connect.
- Thank the teacher for small wins, and tell your child about them too.

## If it goes badly

- Expect a few hard days. Change takes time, and tiredness after school is common.
- Look for patterns: certain lessons, times or noises. Note them in Dayline and share them calmly with the teacher.
- If things have not settled after a few weeks, ask to meet the teacher and the head teacher. Bring your notes and one or two ideas to try.
