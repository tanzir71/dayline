Long journeys mean waiting, crowds, noise, new smells and hours of sitting, often on the busiest holiday days. Planning the trip in small steps helps your child know what comes next.

## 1. Before

- Make a simple picture plan of the journey: leave home, rickshaw or CNG, bus, launch or train, food stop, arrive, who will be there.
- If you can, travel at quieter times, buy tickets ahead, and choose a window or end seat where your child has a little space.
- Pack a travel bag with your child: safe snacks, water, a change of clothes, wipes, plastic bags, headphones, a favourite toy, a charged phone and a power bank.
- Keep any medicines your child takes in your hand bag, not in the luggage, with the prescription.
- Put a card with your child's name and your phone number in their pocket or on a wristband.

## 2. On the way

- Tell your child each step as it comes: "Now we wait. Next the bus. Then we eat."
- Show progress, like ticking off stops on paper or counting stations, so the trip has a clear end.
- Offer headphones before the horns and announcements start. Let your child look out, sleep or use the phone.
- Take breaks where you can: walk on the launch deck, stretch at a stop, take a short walk in the station.
- Keep food and drink regular, even if your child eats little. Hunger and thirst make everything harder.

## 3. When you arrive

- Give your child a quiet corner in the new place, with familiar things from home: their pillow, blanket or cup.
- Keep bedtime close to the usual time, with the usual steps.
- Note what helped on the way, so the trip back is easier.

## If it goes badly

- Stop if you can: get off at the next stop, go out on the deck, or pull over somewhere safe.
- Use fewer words and less noise, and offer what calms your child (Help now in Dayline has the steps).
- If your child gets lost, tell staff or the police straight away and show a recent photo. In an emergency, call your emergency number (999 in Bangladesh).
