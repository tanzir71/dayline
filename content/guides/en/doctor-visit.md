Clinics are hard for many autistic children: long waits, bright lights, strangers, being touched, and not knowing what comes next. A little planning can make the visit shorter and give the doctor a clearer picture.

## 1. Before

- Tell your child what will happen, in order, with photos or simple drawings: waiting room, name called, doctor's room, the check-up, home.
- Ask for the first appointment of the day, or the time the clinic is quietest. Ask if you can wait outside or in the car until it is your turn.
- Write down your questions and the main changes since the last visit: sleep, food, toilet, hard moments, and any medicines from other doctors.
- Pack a bag: water, a snack your child accepts, a comfort object, headphones, a charged phone, and old prescriptions and reports.
- Practise at home with a toy doctor kit: listen to the heart, look in the ears, press gently on the tummy.

## 2. At the clinic

- Wait somewhere quieter if you can: a corridor, outside, or a corner away from the TV.
- Tell the doctor briefly what helps your child: "Please tell her before you touch her" or "He understands best with fewer words."
- Ask the doctor to say each step before doing it, and to show each tool first.
- Let your child sit on your lap or hold your hand for the check-up. Agree with the doctor on what matters most if not everything can be done today.
- Hand over your list of questions so nothing is forgotten in the rush.

## 3. After

- Keep the promise you made: the snack, the video, the trip home.
- Write down what the doctor said and any next steps before you forget. If anything is unclear, ask the doctor or pharmacist to explain again.
- Note in Dayline what helped with the wait and the check-up, so the next visit starts from there.

## If it goes badly

- It is fine to stop and ask for a break, or to step outside and come back.
- Ask if the check-up can be done in a different way, or on another day.
- If your child is in pain or unwell and the visit cannot go ahead, ask the clinic what to do next. If it is urgent, go to a hospital emergency department.
