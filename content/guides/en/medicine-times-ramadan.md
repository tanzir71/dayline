When the family's day changes in Ramadan, it can be tempting to move a child's medicine times to fit, but only your child's prescriber can say whether any change is safe. Dayline never suggests doses or changes; ask the prescriber.

## 1. Before you ask

- List every medicine your child takes, from every doctor: the name, the strength on the label, and the times it is given now. Include anything bought without a prescription.
- Note how your child's day will change: meal times, sleep, school, travel for Eid.
- Book a visit or call with the prescriber a few weeks before Ramadan, so there is time to plan.

## 2. Questions to ask the prescriber

- "Our meal and sleep times will change in Ramadan. Should my child's medicine times stay the same, or change?"
- "Does this medicine need to be taken with food, or on an empty stomach? What does that mean for our new meal times?"
- "If a dose is late or missed, what should I do?"
- "If my child wants to try fasting, is that safe with their medicines and health?"
- "What signs should I watch for, and when should I call you?"

## 3. After the visit

- Write down the answers in the prescriber's words, and ask them to write any new times on the prescription.
- Tell everyone who gives medicines, such as grandparents, sitters or school, so everyone follows the same plan.
- If you use reminders in Dayline, set them to the times the prescriber gave you.

## When to get help

- If you are unsure about a dose or a time, ask the prescriber or a pharmacist before giving it.
- If your child seems unwell, very sleepy, or different after any change, contact the prescriber.
- Danger signs, like trouble breathing, a seizure, or being very hard to wake: go to a hospital emergency department or call 999.
