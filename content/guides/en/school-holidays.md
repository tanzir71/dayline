When school stops, the shape of the day goes with it. Many autistic children find empty days harder than busy ones, so a simple, predictable holiday plan can help everyone.

## 1. Before the holiday

- Mark the holiday on a calendar your child can see, with the first day back clearly shown.
- Make a picture plan for a typical holiday day: wake up, breakfast, activity, lunch, rest, outing, dinner, bedtime.
- Ask the teacher for a few school-like activities, or the class routine, to use at home.
- Add any big events (visits, travel, weddings, Victory Day) to the calendar ahead of time.

## 2. During the holiday

- Keep wake-up, meal and bedtime steps close to school days. The time can be a little later; keep the order.
- Plan one main activity or outing a day, followed by quiet time at home.
- Mix movement and calm: a walk, the roof, a park when it is quiet, then drawing, puzzles, building or a favourite show.
- Give your child jobs they enjoy: watering plants, sorting clothes, helping to cook.
- Warn about changes as early as you can, and show them on the plan.

## 3. Going back to school

- Count down the last few days on the calendar.
- A few days before, move back to school wake-up and bedtimes.
- Look at photos of the school, the teacher and the uniform. Pack the bag together the night before.
- Tell the teacher about anything new from the holiday, and update the brief if something has changed.

## If it goes badly

- Make the plan simpler: fewer outings, more of what your child enjoys at home.
- A day at home with a favourite routine is a fine holiday day.
- If hard moments increase, think about what changed (sleep, food, noise, visitors) and note it in Dayline. Use the Help now steps when you need them.
