New clothes can feel scratchy, tight, stiff or strange-smelling, and labels and seams can really hurt. Festival outfits often add sequins, embroidery and stiff fabric, and your child's comfort matters more than the look.

## 1. Choosing

- Let your child touch and choose, or pick between two or three options you are happy with.
- Look for soft cotton, few seams, no labels or printed labels, and loose necks and waists with soft elastic.
- Choose something close to what your child already wears happily: the same cut, colour or fabric.
- For Eid or Pohela Boishakh, choose one special piece (a panjabi, a kurta, a frock) and keep the rest familiar.

## 2. Getting used to them

- Wash new clothes two or three times before wearing to soften them and remove the shop smell.
- Cut out labels carefully, and check inside for rough seams, threads, sequins or beads that scratch.
- Try the clothes at home for a few minutes, during something your child enjoys. Make it a little longer each day.
- Show photos of the family in festival clothes, and say when your child will wear theirs.
- A soft vest or T-shirt underneath can protect the skin from embroidery or stiff fabric.

## 3. On the day

- Dress your child just before you leave, not hours ahead.
- Bring a familiar change of clothes in the bag.
- Agree on how long: "New clothes for the photos and lunch, then your blue T-shirt."

## If it goes badly

- Let it go. Your child can wear familiar clothes to the festival; family will understand, and you can explain if needed.
- Note which fabrics, cuts and colours worked in Dayline, so next time is easier.
- If your child suddenly refuses clothes they used to wear, check their skin for rashes, bites or sore spots, and see a doctor if something looks wrong.
