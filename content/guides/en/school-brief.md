A good brief helps a teacher understand your child from the first day, instead of learning the hard way. Keep it short, kind and practical: one page is enough.

## 1. What to put in the brief

- Start with your child: their name, what they love, what they are good at, and what makes them laugh.
- How your child communicates: words, signs, pictures, a device, or behaviour. What "yes", "no" and "I need a break" look like.
- What is hard: noise, crowds, changes, certain textures or smells, waiting, the toilet.
- Early signs of overload, and what helps: a quiet corner, headphones, a movement break, fewer words.
- Practical things: toilet needs, food, any medicine given at school (as the doctor prescribed it), and who to call.

## 2. Sharing it

- Ask for a short meeting before term starts, or in the first week, with the class teacher and anyone else who helps.
- Bring the brief on paper, or send it as a PDF on WhatsApp, in Bangla or English as the school prefers. Dayline's brief can make this page for you.
- Bring a recent photo of your child. If it helps, take photos of the classroom and teacher to show your child at home.
- Agree how you will hear about the day: a note in the bag, a WhatsApp message, or a quick word at pickup.

## 3. In the first weeks

- Check in after a few days. Ask what is going well first.
- Update the brief when something changes: a new medicine, a hard week at home, something new that helps.
- Thank the teacher for what works. Teachers who feel trusted often try more.

## If it goes badly

- Ask for a calm meeting. Start from what you both want: your child safe, learning and settled.
- Bring notes of what happened and when, and suggest one or two small changes to try.
- If the school will not make reasonable changes, the guide on rights in Bangladesh explains where to ask.
