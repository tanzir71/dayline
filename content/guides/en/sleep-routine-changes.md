Holidays, Ramadan, guests and travel shift meal times, bedtimes and noise at home. Many autistic children sleep best with the same steps every night, so keeping the steps steady matters more than the exact time.

## 1. Before the change

- Look ahead: what will change, and for how long? Tell your child with a calendar or pictures.
- If bedtime has to move, shift it slowly, about 15 minutes every few days, rather than all at once.
- Write down your child's bedtime steps in order (bath, clothes, teeth, story, lights out) so anyone helping can follow them.
- Plan where your child will sleep if guests come or you travel. Bring their pillow, blanket or night light.

## 2. During the change

- Keep the same steps in the same order, even on late nights. A shorter version is fine.
- Get morning light and some movement every day: a walk, the roof, the yard.
- Keep the bedroom dark, cool and quiet. A fan or a soft, steady sound can cover voices from guests or late meals.
- Where you can, keep screens and heavy snacks away from the last hour before sleep.
- In Ramadan, if the house wakes for sehri, decide ahead whether your child stays asleep, and keep their room as dark and quiet as you can.

## 3. Getting back on track

- After the holiday, go back to the usual wake-up time first. Bedtime usually follows within a few days.
- Bring back the usual day: meal times, school or therapy, time outdoors.
- Note how sleep went in Dayline. It helps you see what worked and plan for next time.

## When to get help

- If poor sleep lasts more than a few weeks, or your child is very tired during the day, talk to your child's doctor.
- Ask the doctor before giving anything to help sleep, including products sold without a prescription.
- Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or gasping at night, is worth telling the doctor about soon.
