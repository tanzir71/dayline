Finding assessment, therapy and support can take time, and waiting is hard. This guide describes the kinds of places families in Bangladesh often use, so you know where to start asking.

## 1. Kinds of help

- Child development centres (Shishu Bikash Kendra) in many government medical college hospitals offer assessment and follow-up for children who develop differently.
- The Institute of Paediatric Neurodisorder & Autism (IPNA) at Bangladesh Medical University in Dhaka offers assessment and therapy services.
- Disability service and help centres (Protibondhi Sheba O Sahajjo Kendro), run by the Jatiyo Protibondhi Unnayan Foundation in many districts, offer therapy and other support.
- Parent support groups and autism organisations, in person or online, where parents share what has worked locally.
- Private therapists and clinics: speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, special education. Ask about their training and qualifications.

## 2. Getting started

- Start with who you already see: your child's doctor or a nearby government hospital can refer you.
- Gather a folder: birth registration, earlier reports, prescriptions, school notes, and a short list of what you have noticed and since when.
- Write down your three main questions before each visit.
- Check opening days and fees before you go. Ask whether you need a referral or an appointment, and how long the wait is.

## 3. While you wait

- Guides in Dayline, Help now, and a simple daily routine at home are useful now, before any assessment.
- Notes about sleep, food, toilet, hard moments and wins will help whoever sees your child.
- Talk to other parents. They often know which services are running and how to reach them.

## 4. Be careful with

- Anyone who promises to cure autism, or sells costly diets, supplements, injections or devices to "fix" it. Ask your child's doctor first.
- Any "treatment" that hurts, frightens or restrains your child. Help should feel safe to your child.

## When to get help

- If you are struggling, tell your own doctor. Caring is hard work, and you deserve support too.
- In an emergency, call 999.
