Family visits bring new faces, lots of talk, hugs, strong smells and food your child may not eat. Relatives usually mean well, and a little preparation helps your child cope and helps them understand.

## 1. Before

- Show photos of the people and the house. Say who will be there and how long you will stay.
- Tell one or two close relatives what helps: "He may not say salaam or look at you, but he is happy you are there." Ask them to pass it on.
- Ask if there is a quiet room your child can use, and how busy it will be: TV, children, guests.
- Pack food your child already eats, water, a comfort object, headphones and a charged phone or tablet.
- Plan shorter visits. Two short visits often go better than one long day.

## 2. During the visit

- Arrive before the house fills up, so your child can settle and explore first.
- Let your child greet in their own way: a wave, a high five, or just being there. Step in kindly if someone insists on a hug or a kiss.
- If people press food, keep it simple: "She has her own food today, thank you."
- Take breaks in the quiet room before your child shows signs of overload, not after.
- Keep the familiar parts of the day where you can: the same snack time, the same video, the same nap.

## 3. After

- Give your child quiet time at home, with low noise and a familiar activity.
- Thank the relatives who helped, and tell them what worked. It makes the next visit easier.
- Note what helped in Dayline so you can plan the next Eid from there.

## If it goes badly

- Move to the quiet room or outside, use fewer words, and offer what calms your child (Help now in Dayline has the steps).
- Leave early if you need to. You can explain later; your child comes first.
- If comments hurt, you do not have to answer on the spot. A short "We are doing what works for him" is enough.
