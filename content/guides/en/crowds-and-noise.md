Markets, melas, rallies, horns and fireworks can be too much for many autistic children: loud, bright, crowded and hard to predict. You can keep the outing short, plan a way out, and bring what helps.

## 1. Before

- Ask yourself if your child needs to go. Sometimes a shorter visit, a quieter time, or staying home with someone they trust is the kinder choice.
- Show photos or videos of the place. Talk through what will happen and when you will leave.
- Pack ear defenders or headphones, sunglasses or a cap, water, a snack and a favourite object.
- Agree on a simple break sign, like a card, a hand signal or a word, that means "I need to get out now."
- For fireworks nights (Victory Day, Pohela Boishakh, weddings), close windows, draw the curtains, and plan a fan, music or a video at home.

## 2. While you are out

- Go early, before it fills up, and find your quiet spot first: a shop corner, the car, or a relative's home nearby.
- Put ear defenders on before it gets loud, not after.
- Hold hands in crowds. Put a card with your phone number in your child's pocket.
- Watch for early signs: covering ears, going quiet, pulling away, faster movements. Take a break at the first sign.
- Keep your words few and calm. Point to the way out instead of explaining.

## 3. After

- Plan quiet time at home: dim light, low noise, a familiar activity.
- Expect a tired or unsettled evening. Keep bedtime simple.
- Note what helped and how long your child managed, so the next outing can be planned around it.

## If it goes badly

- Leave. Going home early is not a failure.
- Move to the quietest place you can reach and use what calms your child (Help now in Dayline shows the steps).
- Next time, try a shorter visit, a quieter time, or a different way to join in, like watching from a rooftop or a window.
